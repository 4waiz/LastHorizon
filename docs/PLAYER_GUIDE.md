# Player guide

*A Kanban Studios game — kanbanstudios.ae. Game Developer: Awaiz Ahmed.*

**No spoilers.** Where the story goes is in
[NARRATIVE_GUIDE.md](NARRATIVE_GUIDE.md), which is split so the first half is
safe to read.

---

## The short version

You are fifteen, in a coastal village with one road through it. The road goes
somewhere. Over about ten to fourteen hours you find out where, and what it
costs to follow it.

**One hour of playing is one year of your life.** At eighteen the city opens.
The story runs to twenty-five, and then you keep playing.

## Starting

Open the link. There is no launcher, no account and no download.

**Story Mode** starts at fifteen in the village. **Free Roam** lets you pick a
starting age, how much money you have, whether you already own a vehicle,
which places are unlocked, and whether you age at all. It is called Free Roam
rather than "free to play" because there is nothing to buy — no purchases, no
ads, no currency, ever.

Everything saves to your own device. Three slots and an autosave.

## The first hour

Nobody will hurry you. If you would rather just walk around the village and
look at it, that is a legitimate way to play.

When you want a thread to pull:

1. **Find the five keepsakes.** They are around the village and the counter
   top-right tells you how many are left. This is the prologue.
2. **Ask Maryam at the grocery for work.** A shift is forty-five dollars,
   which is most of a comfortable day.
3. **Eat something.** Hunger, energy, cleanliness and mood drift down slowly.
   At their worst you run about 15% slower and nothing else happens — they are
   texture, not a survival mechanic. Turn any of them off in ⓘ → Needs.
4. **Get a bicycle.** Buy one, or fix the dead one behind Tomás's shed.

## Money

Whole dollars, no cents. One shift is about one comfortable day with something
left over — which is deliberately tight with one job, and comfortably positive
if you take the harder work.

| Job | Pays | Timed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grocery shift | $45 | no |
| Parcel round | $30 | no |
| City courier | $55 | 4 min |
| Taxi shift | $12 a fare | 5 min |
| Recovery call | $70 | no |
| Fishing | in fish | no |

Jobs get better paid the more you do them — every fourth *completed* run moves
the rate up. Failing does not make a job harder, which would be the wrong way
round.

Rent on the city room is $180 every seven days, charged when you sleep there.
If you cannot pay it goes overdue and nothing else happens: there is no
eviction and no lockout.

Selling always pays less than buying. There is no way to farm the shop, and
there is a test that proves it.

## Growing up

Birthdays pause the game, show you a card and hand you whatever the year
unlocks. They fire once — reloading will not replay one.

- **17** — driving.
- **18** — the city, adult work, and the optional systems below.

## The city

At eighteen you take the bus. The city is three districts: Old Market,
Downtown and the Waterfront. Rent a room, take a job, and the rest is up to
you.

**A warning worth having:** the districts are streets, traffic and people, and
they have no enterable buildings yet. The nine you can go inside are all in
the village. This is the largest gap in the release and it is written down in
[KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md](KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md) rather than left for you to find.

## The optional half

From eighteen there are weapons, theft and police. **You never have to touch
any of it.** The story can be finished, to a proper ending, without committing
a single crime — and an automated run proves that on every commit.

If you do:

- **Police are not omniscient.** They respond when they see something, when a
  witness reaches help, or when they find something you left behind. Hiding
  works because a cold trail decays.
- **Heat runs 0 to 5.** You can always surrender, and it costs what being
  caught costs — a few hours, a fine, and your vehicle in the pound.
- **Nobody dies.** There is no health bar. People lose their composure, sit
  down, and recover. No blood, no gore, and no child is ever a target.

## Getting around

A bicycle, a scooter, a hatchback, a van — and, once you find the airstrip, a
small aeroplane. Flying is forgiving by default: it levels the wings when you
let go, will not let you stall, and flares for you on landing. If you would
rather it did not, ⓘ → Accessibility → Flight assist.

If a vehicle ends up somewhere impossible, the garage will get it back.

## When you get lost

- **M** — map. **J** — journal, which says what you are meant to be doing.
  **P** — phone: work, people, garage.
- The radar bottom-left rotates with you. Gold pulses are keepsakes you have
  not found.
- Shops have opening hours and the door will tell you when to come back.

Full bindings: [CONTROLS.md](CONTROLS.md).

## Making it comfortable

ⓘ, top right.

- **Text size** up to 1.6×, which scales the whole interface.
- **Reduced motion**, following your system or overriding it.
- **High-contrast prompts.**
- **Heat as a numeral**, so the wanted level does not depend on seeing colour
  or counting position.
- **Flashes off**, for photosensitivity.
- **Aim assist** and **camera shake**.
- **Needs** individually off, or at half speed.

## Your save

Three slots and an autosave, on your device, in your browser. Export one to a
file to keep it or move it; import brings it back.

**Clearing your browser's site data deletes your saves.** There is no cloud
backup, by design — no accounts, nothing leaves your machine. Export anything
you would be sad to lose.

## Playing offline

After your first visit the village is cached and the game will start with no
network at all. A quiet bar tells you when you are offline; nothing else
changes, because nothing in this game ever needed a server.

When a new version is out you get an offer, not an interruption — it waits
until you press Update, because reloading in the middle of a mission is rude.

## The end, and after it

Around twenty-five the story resolves into one of three broad endings, with
variants that read what you did rather than what you said. Then you get a
**Life Reel**: a card that answers *what did you become by 25?* You can save it
as an image, drawn on your machine and downloaded to it. There is nowhere to
upload it to, and that is on purpose.

After the credits, Free Roam continues — with ageing on, slowed, or stopped.
